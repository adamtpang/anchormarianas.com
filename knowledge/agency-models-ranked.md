# How AI service businesses are structured, ranked by profit per hour of founder time

Provenance: web research 2026-07-25 plus prior peer research in memory (anchor_guam_ai_playbook). Third-party figures, confirm before quoting.

## The premise correction

The most profitable "AI companies" are product companies, not agencies. Cursor runs roughly $2B ARR on about 50 staff (~$40M revenue per employee). Anthropic ~$45B ARR, OpenAI ~$33B, Sierra hit $100M ARR in 7 quarters. None of these are agencies and none of their playbooks transfer to a solo consultancy.

Meanwhile the agency model, selling hours and headcount, has the worst economics in the category: 20 to 40 percent gross margins industry-average, and consulting margins are actively shrinking in 2026. Revenue per employee in mid-market professional services is $150K to $250K, versus Cursor's ~$40M. That is a 160x difference in leverage.

So "how do I structure Anchor as an AI agency" contains a trap. Agency is the least profitable structure available.

## The four structures, ranked

| Rank | Structure | Economics | Growth requires | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Product** | ~$40M revenue per employee | code, not people | Cursor, Sierra |
| 2 | **Productized service** (fixed price, fixed scope, templated) | 50 to 70% gross margin for high performers | templates and demand | Morningside, Smith.ai |
| 3 | **Consultancy** (custom, expert, hourly) | $150 to $1,000/hr, high margin per hour | founder hours | boutique AI advisories |
| 4 | **Agency** (sell hours, add headcount) | 20 to 40% gross margin, shrinking | hiring | traditional dev shops |

The solo-plus-AI ceiling is roughly $1M to $3M ARR before you must hire. 38 percent of seven-figure businesses are now solopreneur-led, so a one-person seven-figure services business is a documented, realistic target.

## Who to actually study, ranked by copyability

**Tier 1, copy the structure directly (same shape as Anchor):**
- **Smith.ai**: ONE wedge service sold narrow (AI receptionist), $10M+ ARR. The lesson is the narrowness, not the service.
- **Morningside**: productized fixed prices, no custom quotes, no scoping drag.
- **Left Click**: niche-or-die. Dominate one vertical, then ride referrals inside it.
- **Chris Kerner**: free asset as the foot in the door, upsell into recurring care.

**Tier 2, copy the funnel mechanic:**
- **Vendasta**: diagnose-first. Snapshot report, then sell the gaps it exposes. AnchorScan is already this.
- **Dan Martell**: the $5k audit that becomes the $50k implementation. The audit fee credits toward the build.

**Tier 3, the destination, not the starting point:**
- **Cursor, Sierra**: what "one build resold across a vertical" eventually becomes if the template hardens into a product.

## The three traits every profitable one shares

1. **ONE service, sold narrow.** Not a menu of eight.
2. **ONE vertical.** Not "small businesses."
3. **Recurring revenue.** Not one-off builds.

## Where Anchor currently violates all three

- Menu has 8+ items (scan, audit, landing, MVP, receptionist, responder, care, fractional, custom builds).
- Targets all Guam SMBs across 15 verticals.
- Revenue mix is mostly one-off ($497, $1,997, $1,500) with recurring as an afterthought.

This is not a fatal problem, it is the normal pre-revenue state: you keep options open until the market picks one. But the moment a client says yes to something, that is the signal to collapse the menu around it.

## The structural recommendation

Anchor is not an agency and should not be framed as one. It is a **productized AI studio**: fixed prices, fixed timelines, repeatable builds, delivered by the practitioner.

The ladder that gets to product margins without quitting services:
1. Sell the same fixed-price build repeatedly inside ONE vertical.
2. The repetition hardens into a template (this is the "reuse" axis in the ICP index, where Dental and Trades both score 5).
3. The template becomes a product you can sell without your hours.

Services fund the product. The product is built out of the service work already done. That is the only path from 40% margins to product margins that does not require raising money.
