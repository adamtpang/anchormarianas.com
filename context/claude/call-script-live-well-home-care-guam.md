## The framing (read this before dialing)

**Do not open with an acquisition pitch.** A 16-month-old LLC's owner is not
a retirement-exit prospect, and leading with "want to sell?" to someone who
may have just built this would be tone-deaf and would burn a genuinely
useful relationship for no reason. This matches Brad Jacobs' own explicit
method from the book you read: patient, pleasant, polite, never play hard to
get, never renegotiate after a handshake. The corollary that matters here:
**don't make an offer before there's a relationship to make it in.**

The actual goal of this call is threefold, in priority order:
1. **Learn.** She's an active operator in the exact market you're studying.
   What's actually happening in Guam's non-medical home care market:
   demand, staffing, pricing, competitors, regulatory friction. That's
   information you cannot get from a search engine.
2. **Build a relationship.** If she's not a seller today, she may be one in
   3-5 years, or she may know who is (other owners, a supplier, a
   competitor thinking about retiring). Owners talk to other owners.
3. **Signal you're serious and local.** You're Guam-based, thinking about
   this market long-term. Being a known, friendly, non-threatening
   presence now is worth more than a single transactional call.

## The call

**Dial:** 866-448-6565, ask for Tanya (Willie-Tanya Gogue-Eubanks) by name.

**Opening:**

> "Hi, my name is Adam Pangelinan, I run Anchor Marianas here on Guam. I
> heard you on The Brightside talking about Live Well's veteran care
> program, and I've been doing some research into Guam's home care market.
> I'm not in the industry myself, but I'm exploring it as a founder. Do you
> have a few minutes? I'd love to just learn from your experience running
> the business, no pitch, I promise."

That last line matters. Naming that you're not selling her anything, up
front, earns you the real conversation.

**Questions, if she has time (adapt to how the conversation actually goes;
don't run this as a rigid checklist):**

1. What's it actually like running a home care business on Guam right now:
   what's the hardest part day to day? (staffing, client acquisition,
   regulatory, cash flow; let her lead)
2. How did Live Well get started, and how's it grown since?
3. What do you see as the biggest gap in Guam's senior/home care market
   right now: where's demand outpacing supply?
4. Are there other operators on Guam you'd point me to, if I wanted to
   understand the space better? (This is how you find out about Guam
   Visiting Nurses, Guam Home Care, Care Connect, and anyone not yet on
   your radar, from someone inside the industry, which is far better
   signal than a web search.)
5. If it comes up naturally, and only if it does: is she open to staying in
   touch as you keep exploring this space? Don't force a "would you ever
   sell" question onto this call: let a second or third conversation earn
   that, if it's ever relevant at all.

**If she asks what Anchor Marianas does or what your angle is:** be
straightforward: you're exploring building something in the Guam senior
care / home care space, still early, learning first. True, and it doesn't
overclaim or create a promise you're not ready to make.
