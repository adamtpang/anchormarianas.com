# Call script: Visiting Angels corporate, Hawaii territory

Prepped 2026-08-05 for the silver-generation rollup thesis
(`session-2026-08-05-silver-generation-rollup.md`). Purpose: find out what
happened to Visiting Angels' Hawaii territory and, ideally, get a name to call
directly. This is intelligence-gathering, not a franchise application.

## What's confirmed before dialing (researched 2026-08-05, sourced)

- **Visiting Angels no longer operates in Hawaii.** Their own site says so
  directly: "we no longer offer services in Hawaii at this time"
  ([visitingangels.com/senior-home-care-hawaii-hi](https://www.visitingangels.com/senior-home-care-hawaii-hi)).
- **There was a real operating location before this.** Stale directory
  listings (Yelp, Mapquest, Care.com, an Instagram handle
  `@visitingangelshawaii`) show two addresses that were never updated after
  the exit:
  - 99-149 Moanalua Rd, Ste 201, Aiea, HI 96701: (808) 888-3509
  - 1016 Hao St, Honolulu, HI 96821: (808) 377-5264
  These numbers may be dead, may forward, or may reach the former owner
  directly: worth trying before or after the corporate call.
- **The territory map's current "new/available territory" list does not
  show a Hawaii listing today**
  ([franchise.visitingangels.com/territory-map](https://franchise.visitingangels.com/territory-map)),
  so it's not actively marketed as open, which either means it's not
  formally reverted yet, or it's open but just unlisted. Worth asking
  directly.
- **Main line: 800-365-4189.** This is the number both the consumer site and
  the franchise-development site (`franchise.visitingangels.com`) point to:
  there's no separate direct-dial found for franchise development; you ask
  the main line to route you.
- **If the conversation turns to "so are you interested in a franchise,"**
  know the real numbers going in: initial investment $125,460–$171,150,
  franchise fee $51,950–$89,950 (population-based; ~$64,950 for a 200k-pop
  territory), royalty 3.5%/month of gross sales. Source:
  [1851franchise.com franchise cost breakdown, from the 2025 FDD](https://1851franchise.com/visiting-angels-franchise-costs-fees-profit-2731097).
  That's a *start a new location* number, not what you're actually after:
  the thesis is buying an existing operator (SBA 7(a), seller-financed), not
  starting a Visiting Angels franchise from zero. Good to have the figures so
  you're not caught flat, but don't let the call get redirected into their
  franchise-sales funnel.

## The call

**Dial:** 800-365-4189, ask for **Franchise Development**.

**Opening (say this, don't wing it):**

> "Hi, I'm looking into the home care market in Hawaii and I noticed
> Visiting Angels doesn't have an active location there anymore. I'm
> exploring an acquisition of an existing non-medical home care business in
> Hawaii, not really applying for a new franchise, but I had a couple of
> quick questions about what happened with your Hawaii territory. Do you
> have a minute, or is there someone on the franchise development team who'd
> know the history there?"

This framing does two things: it's honest (avoids wasting their time or
yours on a franchise-sales pitch you don't want), and it signals you're a
market participant worth talking to, not a random inquiry.

**Questions, in priority order, stop once you have what you need:**

1. When did the Hawaii location(s) close, and do you know why (retirement,
   sale, sold the book of business, just shut down)?
2. Was there a single owner/operator across both the Aiea and Honolulu
   addresses, or two separate franchisees?
3. Do you know if the owner sold the business, or wound it down? If sold,
   any idea who bought it or what it became?
4. Is the Hawaii territory formally available again, or still tied up
   (e.g., in a franchise agreement wind-down, ROFR, non-compete)?
5. Is there a way to get in touch with the prior owner directly? (This is
   the actual prize: a real seller-profile lead, Jacobs' "go direct to the
   owner" method.)
6. If they push toward "would you like to open a new Visiting Angels in
   Hawaii," answer honestly: "Possibly down the line, but right now I'm
   focused on acquiring an existing operation. If that changes I'll follow
   up." Don't let them book you into a franchise-discovery call unless you
   actually want that follow-on step.

**If Franchise Development doesn't know anything** (likely: Hawaii closed
enough that this may be old history to them): ask if there's a regional
operations contact from that era, or if they can check FDD historical
records for a prior Hawaii franchisee name. If they can't help further,
that's fine: the call's done its job by ruling this in/out fast.
