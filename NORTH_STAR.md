# North star: the platonic ideal version of the silver-generation rollup

Scoped to Line 2 of MASTERPLAN.md only (the Guam eldercare acquisition
thesis). Line 1 (AI services) has its own separate progress, not tracked
here.

One sentence: when it's perfect, Adam owns and personally operates a
cash-flowing, SBA/seller-financed non-medical home-care business on Guam,
standardized on one platform with an AI-automated back office, proven
enough to be a credible template for a second acquisition.

## The offer

- Who benefits: Adam (owner-operator), Miguel (role still undocumented)
- What they get: a real, cash-flowing acquired business plus a proven,
  repeatable playbook for deal #2, not a rollup on day one, one deal
  proven operationally first
- What it costs: search time (sourcing, relationship-building, financing
  conversations), a 10-20% equity injection, a personal guarantee on the
  SBA debt, and the operating risk of running a licensed care business

## What this is NOT (scope guard)

- Not a rollup yet: one deal, proven, before a second is even discussed
- Not Hawaii-first anymore: Guam is the sourcing market, Hawaii is a
  shelved fallback only
- Not a Medicare-certified or nonprofit target: SBA 7(a) cannot finance
  either, both are disqualifying regardless of how good the business looks
- Not an acquisition pitch on the first call to any operator: relationship
  before ask, per Brad Jacobs' own method
- Not publicly conflated with the AI-services wedge (Line 1): separate
  ICP, separate capital structure, separate story
